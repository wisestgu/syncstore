[Title] SYNCSTORE
Product Description + Product Requirements Document — v3
Hackaholics 7.0 - Digital Transformation - Working Product Definition - Broad Trade Credit Edition
"Turn informal trade into trusted, usable financial data."
Core Product Thesis: SyncStore is a shared trade-credit operating layer for independent wholesalers/distributors and the small businesses they supply. It automatically reconciles payments, maintains a shared transaction record, handles partial payments and disputes, and turns repeated trade activity into useful behavioural signals for credit decisions.
[Heading1] Build-Lock Decisions (New in v3)
Four open questions from v1 are resolved here so the team can start building without stalling on strategy mid-build. Each is a provisional call the team should be able to defend, not a claim of certainty - swap any of these if real interviews this week point elsewhere.
1. Target market: Independent wholesalers/distributors across fragmented, credit-based trade - including FMCG, building materials, pharmaceuticals, agricultural inputs, electronics, spare parts, hospitality supplies, and other B2B wholesale categories - supplying small retailers, contractors, or business customers. This is deliberately horizontal: SyncStore is infrastructure for the trade-credit relationship, not a vertical-specific operating system. The demo should use whichever sector the team can validate most strongly; the ledger logic does not change.
2. Messaging: Simulated/mocked WhatsApp payloads only, for the entire build - not "simulation as fallback." Real WhatsApp Business API approval realistically takes days the team doesn't have; zero hours should go toward chasing it before the demo.
3. Trust Score: The six-factor weighted formula in Part II Section 9 is the only scoring mechanism. No model is trained. Machine learning, where used at all, is confined to the anomaly-alert layer (a statistical threshold on velocity change), which is easier to explain under judge questioning than "the model learned it."
4. Real-time updates: Polling-based dashboard refresh (2-3 second interval) is the P0 requirement. True WebSocket/SSE push is a P2 stretch goal only if the core flow is done early - a judge cannot tell the difference in a two-minute demo, and it removes a whole class of hackathon-week bugs.

[Heading1] Part I - Product Description
[Heading2] 1. What SyncStore Is
SyncStore is not a general accounting app, ERP, marketplace, or another payment gateway. It is a focused infrastructure layer for the financial relationship between an independent wholesaler/distributor and the small retailers or contractor customers they repeatedly supply.
The product sits between an existing commercial relationship and the fragmented records created around it. The distributor keeps their existing customers and supply chain. Retailers do not need to join a new marketplace. SyncStore simply makes the relationship easier to record, reconcile, monitor, and trust.
[Heading2] 2. The Problem
[ListParagraph] An independent wholesaler/distributor supplies dozens of small retailers, contractors, or business customers. Each customer can have multiple purchases, partial repayments, outstanding balances, disputes over quantity or quality, and different payment moments - often tied to when their own customers or clients pay them.
[ListParagraph] The underlying evidence is scattered across bank alerts, receipts, sales representatives, notebooks, spreadsheets, WhatsApp messages, and memory.
[ListParagraph] The distributor has to reconstruct simple facts manually: what was sold, what was paid, what remains outstanding, and whether a dispute (short delivery, damaged goods, wrong quantity) has been resolved.
[ListParagraph] The same fragmented history makes it harder to decide, consistently, which contractors and retailers deserve more trade credit heading into a busy season.
[Heading2] 3. The Insight
The real opportunity is not "automated reconciliation" by itself. Reconciliation is the entry point. The deeper problem is that informal commercial activity generates valuable financial behaviour every day, but that behaviour is not captured as one trusted, reusable record.
SyncStore transforms a series of informal interactions into a structured relationship record: sale -> obligation -> payment -> exception -> resolution -> behavioural history.
[Heading2] 4. The Product Promise
[ListParagraph] For distributors: know who owes you what, without manually reconstructing the story.
[ListParagraph] For retailers/contractors: have a clear, shared record of what you owe and what you have already paid.
[ListParagraph] For financial partners: gain structured behavioural data instead of relying only on fragmented transaction evidence.
[Heading2] 5. Core User Flow
[ListParagraph] Create relationship: distributor adds a retailer/contractor and sets a starting credit relationship or trade account.
[ListParagraph] Record sale: distributor records a sale, invoice, or credit transaction (e.g. 200 cases of drinks, 50 sheets of roofing, or other wholesale inventory). The customer now has an outstanding obligation.
[ListParagraph] Payment arrives: payment is received through an available payment rail. The system receives the payment event.
[ListParagraph] Reconcile: SyncStore matches the payment to the appropriate customer/transaction and updates the shared ledger.
[ListParagraph] Handle exceptions: partial payments, ambiguous payments, disputes (e.g. short delivery), returns, or manual adjustments are recorded explicitly rather than hidden in chat.
[ListParagraph] Communicate: customer receives a simple balance/receipt/status notification via simulated WhatsApp.
[ListParagraph] Learn: as transaction history accumulates, SyncStore produces behavioural indicators and risk alerts for the distributor.
[Heading2] 6. The Shared Ledger
Every distributor-customer relationship has a continuously updated record, showing the original obligation, all payments, allocation decisions, disputes/adjustments, and current balance in one place. Example: Adeyemi Wholesale, supplying Grace Retail & Trading.
[TABLE]
Event | Amount | Status | Evidence
Purchase / invoice - 200 bags cement | NGN 600,000 | Open | Invoice / sale record
Payment | NGN 300,000 | Confirmed | Payment reference + timestamp
Remaining | NGN 300,000 | Outstanding | Derived from ledger
Dispute - 12 bags short on delivery | NGN 36,000 | Under review | Delivery note / manual evidence
[/TABLE]
[Heading2] 7. Zero-UI Retailer Experience
The customer experience is intentionally lightweight - no new app to learn. A retailer or contractor should be able to receive a balance, receipt, or reminder through a familiar channel. For the hackathon build this is a simulated WhatsApp thread with realistic message payloads; the architecture leaves room for a real provider later.
Example message: "Payment received: NGN 300,000. Current outstanding balance: NGN 300,000. View statement."
[Heading2] 8. Handling Messy Commerce
[ListParagraph] Partial payments: a NGN 300,000 payment against a NGN 600,000 obligation is recorded as partial, not rejected as an error.
[ListParagraph] Multiple-invoice payments: a payment can be held for allocation instead of being incorrectly attached to one invoice.
[ListParagraph] Disputes: users can surface the payment reference, amount, timestamp, and transaction context for review - e.g. a short-delivery claim on a wholesale order.
[ListParagraph] Manual adjustments: authorized distributors can record cash collections, returns, credits, or corrections with an audit trail.
[ListParagraph] Unmatched payments: funds that cannot be confidently assigned are flagged for human review rather than silently misallocated.
[Heading2] 9. Behavioural Intelligence
Once a distributor has sufficient history, SyncStore computes behavioural signals: repayment consistency, average days outstanding, payment-frequency changes, transaction volume, dispute frequency, and recent payment-velocity changes. These are decision-support signals, not automatic loan approvals.
[Heading2] 10. Trust / Credit Score
The Trust Score is a transparent, weighted behavioural index (see Part II Section 9) - never an opaque "AI credit score." The score always exposes the underlying factors so a distributor can see why it changed.
[ListParagraph] Repayment consistency
[ListParagraph] Average repayment duration
[ListParagraph] Late-payment frequency
[ListParagraph] Outstanding exposure
[ListParagraph] Payment-velocity trend
[ListParagraph] Dispute frequency and resolution behaviour
[Heading2] 11. Predictive Risk / Anomaly Detection
The intelligence layer focuses on detecting meaningful changes, not adding a chatbot. Example: a contractor whose normal weekly payment behaviour drops sharply while outstanding exposure increases triggers an alert such as:
"Behavioural change detected: payment velocity down 40% over the last 7 days. Review current credit exposure."
This uses a simple statistical threshold on the velocity trend - not a trained model - so every alert can be explained in one sentence if a judge asks how it was produced.
[Heading2] 12. Why This Is Different
SyncStore explicitly avoids becoming a closed-loop marketplace or a replacement for a distributor's existing commercial relationship. The distributor keeps their customers, products, suppliers, pricing, and ordinary way of doing business. SyncStore adds a neutral record and intelligence layer on top of that relationship - and it deliberately sits as a neutral trade-credit layer rather than as a closed-loop marketplace or distributor replacement.
[ListParagraph] No mandatory marketplace switch.
[ListParagraph] No need for the distributor to replace their whole ERP for the MVP.
[ListParagraph] Customers keep familiar payment and communication behaviour.
[ListParagraph] The product is centered on the bilateral trade-credit relationship, not on owning the entire supply chain.
[ListParagraph] The key asset is the shared, auditable history and the intelligence derived from it.
[Heading2] 13. Wema Bank Role
Wema is positioned as infrastructure and a potential financial-services partner, not as the reason the product exists. The customer problem exists independently. Wema can add payment rails, account infrastructure, and potentially future financial products once the behavioural record is strong enough.
Implementation note: exact Wema API products, sandbox access, limits, and partner requirements must be confirmed before promising a specific integration in a pitch or demo.
[Heading2] 14. Product Vision
Make informal commercial relationships measurable, verifiable, and financially useful without forcing the participants to abandon how they already do business.

[Heading2] 7. Core Data Model
[ListParagraph] Distributor: organization, users, business metadata.
[ListParagraph] Retailer/Contractor: customer identity, contact channels, account status, credit relationship.
[ListParagraph] CreditAccount: credit limit, current exposure, relationship state.
[ListParagraph] Transaction: sale/credit event, amount, date, terms, source reference.
[ListParagraph] Payment: amount, timestamp, payment reference, channel/source, match status.
[ListParagraph] Allocation: mapping between payment and one or more obligations.
[ListParagraph] Exception: type, status, reason, notes, evidence references, resolution.
[ListParagraph] BehaviourSnapshot: calculated metrics used for score/alerts.
[ListParagraph] MessageEvent: outbound/inbound simulated WhatsApp event.
[ListParagraph] AuditEvent: actor, timestamp, action, before/after context where appropriate.
[Heading2] 8. Business Rules
[ListParagraph] No payment may be counted twice; payment events require an idempotency key or equivalent deduplication mechanism.
[ListParagraph] A partial payment reduces outstanding exposure but does not close the obligation unless the resulting balance reaches zero or an authorized adjustment settles it.
[ListParagraph] An unmatched payment is never auto-assigned when confidence is below a configured threshold.
[ListParagraph] Manual adjustments must be attributable to a specific user and timestamped.
[ListParagraph] Historical ledger events are append-only from the application's logical perspective; corrections create new events rather than rewriting history.
[ListParagraph] Trust Score calculations require the minimum-history threshold in FR-G2 so a new customer is not given a false-precision score.
[ListParagraph] Risk alerts are recommendations for review, not automatic credit decisions.

[Heading2] 10. AI / ML Specification
No model is trained for v2 of the MVP. The Trust Score is entirely the deterministic, weighted formula in Section 9 - fully explainable, no "the model learned it" exposure under judge questioning.
[ListParagraph] Scoring: weighted-factor formula (Section 9), computed directly from ledger data.
[ListParagraph] Anomaly detection: a statistical threshold on payment-velocity trend (e.g. current vs. trailing average), not a trained classifier.
[ListParagraph] Outputs: behavioural score, anomaly flags, plain-language reasons for both.
[ListParagraph] Required UI: show the top factors behind each alert/score change.
[ListParagraph] Demo data: synthetic but internally consistent, with clearly documented assumptions.
[ListParagraph] Safety: never claim the score guarantees default or loan repayment.
[ListParagraph] Optional stretch (P2 only, time-permitting): a lightweight local model (e.g. Gemma 4 e2b) for classifying free-text dispute notes or simulated inbound WhatsApp queries - kept out of the scoring path entirely.

[Heading2] 12. Payment Integration Architecture
Use a provider-agnostic payment adapter so application logic does not depend directly on one bank API. The adapter exposes a normalized event shape - PaymentReceived, PaymentPending, PaymentReversed, PaymentFailed. For the hackathon, the adapter's only implementation is a deterministic simulator; a Wema-specific implementation can plug in later once exact API access is confirmed.

[Heading2] 17. Risks and Mitigations
[TABLE]
Risk | Mitigation
Existing competitors (OmniRetail, Duplo, FieldAssist) | The product is intentionally horizontal and neutral: it does not compete by owning a marketplace or replacing a distributor's supply chain. Vertical selection should be based on the team's strongest validated customer segment and should be pressure-tested before the final pitch.
Low customer adoption | Zero-UI/simulated messaging; distributor-side value is immediate even before any customer engages.
Bad payment matching | Confidence thresholds, idempotency, explicit unmatched queue, human review.
Weak AI claims | Formula-based score, statistical anomaly threshold, factors always shown - no unexplainable model.
Wema API availability | Provider adapter + deterministic simulation - build never depends on it.
Feature creep | MVP protected around ledger, reconciliation, exceptions, and one intelligence layer - see Section 19.
Data quality | Provenance visible; audit events preserved.
Regulated lending claims | Trust Score is never presented as a credit-bureau or automatic lending decision.
[/TABLE]

[Heading2] 23. Pitch Narrative
Problem: Independent wholesalers/distributors sell on trust, but the financial relationship lives across bank alerts, WhatsApp, notebooks, and memory.
Insight: The most valuable missing asset is not another payment app; it is a trusted shared record of the relationship.
Solution: SyncStore reconciles payments automatically, keeps both sides on the same balance, and captures the behavioural history created by repeated trade.
Transformation: Fragmented payments and informal memory become a live ledger and actionable intelligence.
Why now: Digital payment rails exist, but the operational relationship around trade credit remains fragmented across sectors - and many existing platforms either own the transaction marketplace or require businesses to replace substantial parts of their current workflow.
Wema role: Wema can provide infrastructure and, with appropriate approvals, become a financial-services partner on top of the resulting data layer.
Vision: Make informal trade financially visible without forcing businesses to abandon the relationships and workflows that already work for them.
